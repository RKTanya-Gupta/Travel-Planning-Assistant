# Travel Advisories for India (general reference)

Seasonal and safety notes collected by the team. Conditions change, so check official sources
(IMD, state tourism boards, airline and railway notices) close to the travel date.

## Monsoon season (June to September)
The south-west monsoon brings heavy rain to most of India from June to September. Expect flight and train delays, waterlogging in cities such as Mumbai, and landslides on hill roads. Carry rain gear and keep buffer time in the itinerary.

## Goa in the monsoon
In Goa the monsoon runs roughly June to September. The sea is rough, swimming at many beaches is unsafe, red-flag zones must be respected, and most water sports and many beach shacks are closed during this period. October to March is the main tourist season.

## Hill stations and the Himalayas
Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim and Darjeeling are prone to landslides and road closures in the monsoon. In winter (December to February) high passes can close because of snow. Check road status before travelling and avoid driving on mountain roads at night.

## Summer heat (April to June)
North and central India, including Delhi, Rajasthan (Jaipur, Jodhpur) and Agra, can exceed 40 C in May and June. Plan outdoor sightseeing for early morning and evening, drink plenty of water and avoid the midday sun.

## Winter fog in North India (December to January)
Dense fog in Delhi and the Indo-Gangetic plains often delays morning flights and trains in December and January. Prefer afternoon departures and allow buffer time for connections.

## Chennai and Tamil Nadu rains (October to December)
Tamil Nadu, including Chennai, receives most of its rain from the north-east monsoon between October and December, which can cause flooding. Keep plans flexible in this period.

## Permits for restricted areas
Indian citizens need an Inner Line Permit (ILP) to visit Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Mizoram and Manipur. Foreign nationals need a Protected Area Permit for certain border areas, including parts of the North-East, Sikkim and Ladakh. Apply in advance through the official state portals.

## Monument closing days
Many monuments are closed one day a week. For example, the Taj Mahal in Agra is closed on Fridays and the Red Fort in Delhi is closed on Mondays. This planner reads each attraction's weekly-off day from the dataset and does not schedule it on that day.

## Documents and identification
Carry a government photo ID for domestic flights, train travel and hotel check-in. Foreign visitors should carry their passport and visa at all times. Keep digital copies of tickets and bookings.

## Health and safety
Drink bottled or filtered water, use mosquito repellent especially in the monsoon, and carry basic medicines. Use registered taxis or app cabs, keep valuables secure in crowded markets and stations, and dress modestly at religious sites, where shoes must usually be removed. The all-India emergency number is 112.

## Festivals and peak seasons
Hotel prices and crowds rise sharply during Diwali (October/November), Christmas-New Year, Holi (March) and long weekends. Book transport and hotels early for these dates.
